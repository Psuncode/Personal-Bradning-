**Post body** — write the full body content. Minimum 600 words. Answer-first structure. Entity depth signals that Philip runs a real photography business. Use these section headings and write substantive content under each:

**Opening paragraph (no heading):** Establish immediately that this answers the most common client question — what actually happens from inquiry to gallery delivery. Give the core answer in the first paragraph: a photography session is a structured 60–90 minute collaboration followed by 6–8 hours of editing and a private digital gallery delivered within 2 weeks.

**## The Package Structure**
Explain how Philip structures photography packages:
- Entry-level portrait: 1 hour session, ~20 edited final images — for headshots, LinkedIn photos, individual portraits
- Mid-tier portrait/event: 2 hours, ~40 final images — for couples, small groups, half-day event coverage
- Commercial/event coverage: 3+ hours, 60+ images — for team photos, startup events, commercial product work
Explain WHY the tiers exist — it is about matching delivery expectations to use case, not upselling. A LinkedIn headshot needs 5 perfect images; a family reunion needs 80+ coverage shots. Over-booking session time produces worse results because subjects get fatigued.

**## What Actually Happens Day-Of**
Walk through the timeline of a typical 90-minute portrait session:
- 0–15 min: Warmup. Walk the location, test lighting conditions, let the subject get comfortable. No formal posing yet. Philip uses this time to read the ambient light and set camera settings.
- 15–60 min: Directed portrait work. Philip calls specific poses, adjusts positions, reads light. Subject does not need to think about what to do — they just follow direction.
- 60–75 min: Candid and lifestyle shots. Lower pressure, more natural frames. Often the best images come from this window.
- 75–90 min: Review and wrap. Quick preview of a few hero shots on the camera LCD to give immediate confidence in the results.

**## Pricing Rationale**
Be honest about what drives cost. The biggest variable is not the gear or the hour — it is the editing time. A 90-minute shoot produces 400–600 raw frames. Culling to the best 20% takes 1–2 hours. Editing the selects to final delivery-grade — exposure correction, color grading, skin tone correction — takes another 2–4 hours. The session fee covers the shoot PLUS approximately 6–8 hours of post-production. That is the real cost structure.

Explain the deposit: it reserves time on Philip's calendar and covers scouting/prep time if the client cancels. It is not punitive — it is structural. Photographers lose revenue when a time slot cannot be resold.

**## Preparing for Your Session**
Practical client prep advice:
- Location: Philip will recommend 2–3 options based on shoot type. Clients can suggest their own. Scout together via a brief call or message exchange before the day.
- Wardrobe: Avoid busy patterns and logos. Solid colors photograph cleanest. Bring 2–3 outfit options for portrait sessions to add variety to the final gallery.
- Timing: Golden hour (1 hour after sunrise, 1 hour before sunset) is ideal for outdoor portraits — warm, directional light that flatters skin tones. Overcast days are Philip's second preference: soft, even diffused light with no harsh shadows.
- What to bring: Water, outfit changes if applicable, any props that are personally significant (books, instruments, items that represent the person's work).

**## After the Shoot**
Describe the delivery workflow: images are imported into Lightroom for culling (selecting the best frames). Editing passes cover exposure balancing, color grading for consistency across the set, skin tone correction, and background cleanup where needed. Delivery is through a private Pixieset gallery — client can download full-resolution files, share a link with family, or order prints directly through the gallery. The gallery is password-protected and stays live for 90 days from delivery.

**## Booking**
Availability and packages are at philipsun.com. Deposits are collected via Stripe at the time of booking. Booking confirmations include a calendar invite for both parties. Questions before booking can go directly to Philip by email.

Close with a brief personal note: Philip has shot BYU campus headshots, startup team events, mountain landscape work, and family portraits. The goal is consistent across all of it — images that look exactly like the person, in the best light they have ever been in.
